About Bias Brain Press

I color. I train AI. I got tired of waiting for someone else to make the thing I actually wanted to buy. The coloring book market has roughly 100,000 titles that all share the same DNA: mandalas, flowers, steam punk girls, and swear words, repeat. I searched for something different and found nothing. So I decided to build it myself, but I had one rule, if I'm doing this, it's going to be a quality product. Not a side hustle. Not a quick throw-together. Something worth the paper it's printed on. The idea sat for two years while I worked my day job training large language models. Then it didn't sit anymore. A cognitive biases coloring book. I'm into philosophy and human behavior. I knew I had a fresh and interesting idea, but didn't think about how difficult it was going to be.

Oh yeah, plus I can't draw.

What I am good at is prompting AI models with meticulously engineered instructions to make them output exactly what I want. Four years of training AI models taught me how to make machines do precise, repeatable work. So I built an app... well, I instructed an LLM to build it. An app that calls the top image generation APIs. I wrote a precise set of system instructions that dictates the style of the art precisely before I even say a word. The app scales to the correct aspect ratio, and exports print-ready resolution. It took about two months of iterating prompts and finding the right model for the job. I rebuilt the app from scratch three times, but finally, I was ready to start working.

Coming up with coloring pages that show a flawed thought process is more difficult than can be understood until you actually do it. It never crossed my mind how hard this would be to get right. The result took months longer than "throw a book together and see if it makes money." But I wasn't looking for a quick test. I was looking for something genuinely interesting and something I'd actually want to color.

This is Bias Brain. Volume 1 is just the start.
